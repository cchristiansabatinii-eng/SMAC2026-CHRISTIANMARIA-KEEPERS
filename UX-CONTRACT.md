# Keepers UX Contract

## Product context

- Audience: multigenerational families using a private, offline-first memory ritual.
- Primary jobs: contribute privately, gather, reveal together, decide what to keep, and revisit kept history.
- Target market: global English-language sprint; no country-specific assumptions.
- Active locales: English UI; Unicode user content.
- Timezone/calendar policy: device-local display, UTC persistence as defined by the existing domain layer.
- Accessibility target: WCAG 2.2 AA principles adapted to Flutter mobile, with 48 logical-pixel interactive touch targets.

## Dataset navigation

- Archive uses a bounded render-all, content-first gallery grouped by year and ordered newest first within each group. Every year uses three equal square columns with 24-pixel horizontal screen edges and 4-pixel row and column gaps; Person and Theme remain independent filters.
- Weekly progress counts only photo-format entries from the same week whose access is Weekly Reveal and whose reveal state remains pending; the target is five.
- Current-week entries never appear as browsable family/archive content. Their author may inspect them in the owner-only Your memories interface, where privacy and pending status remain explicit.
- Empty, filtered-empty, loading, and error states remain distinct and recoverable.

## Navigation and responsive behavior

- Route titles use `{Screen} — Keepers` in platform route semantics where applicable.
- A cold process launch begins immediately with the supplied Keepers wordmark alone on the black `ground` field, using matching optimized native and Flutter assets so the handoff never exposes a blank frame. It then plays `Be present.`, `Be authentic.`, `Keep moments`, and `Be you.` in Modern Society with cream memory forms before dissolving into the already-resolved route. It runs once per app process, never on foreground resume, and a full-screen tap skips without changing routing state. Cold-link and local-identity resolution continue beneath it.
- `StartupGate` requires a current account session before routing a local identity. A bound identity proceeds only when its account matches the session; an unbound identity may proceed while its first owner bootstrap confirms and binds that same account. With no local identity, it requires `Create your Keepers account`, then a confirmed email OTP or Google, Microsoft, or Apple session, before exposing Create or Join family. A mismatch or an account already attached to another cloud family never enters an unrelated family surface: `Use another account` signs out, clears only a server-proven invalid binding when necessary, and returns to the account gate without deleting local memories, keys, family, or member data. The authentication session links membership only; it is not recovery for memories or encryption keys stored on another device.
- The Family Wheel is the owning home route and the sole entry to the Weekly experience. Capture returns to the main destination that opened it; member and owner-memory interfaces are pushed detail routes. The family-code sheet is contextual to the Wheel and opens from either the top-right code or Invite bubble for every active member; only regeneration is creator-only. Join is an incoming contextual route and is never added to primary navigation.
- The first-run Wheel contains only the persisted current member plus Invite. Accepted or otherwise persisted family members use balanced fixed anchors with no rendered orbit, connector, arc, guide, or trail. Families with more than four relatives extend those anchors into static rows within the page's normal vertical scroll so every member remains reachable without manipulating the bubble field. Beneath the bubbles, the order is five-photo Weekly progress, then the standalone `Ask [waiting names] to come` prompt, then the Weekly panel. The prompt nudges absent existing members; for a one-person family with no relatives, `Ask family to come` opens Invite. It is not part of the Weekly panel. Runtime surfaces never substitute sample relatives, demo roster entries, fabricated proximity, or fabricated contribution percentages.
- Only exact `https://join.keepers.app/f/<code>` URLs enter family-link routing. Cold and warm delivery open `FamilyJoinScreen.forCode`; the active route coalesces duplicate OS delivery while a later deliberate reopen remains valid. Malformed URLs inside that namespace show the neutral malformed Join state and unrelated or legacy `keepers://join` URLs are ignored. Android declares an auto-verified `/f/` App Link and both signed iOS entitlement files request `applinks:join.keepers.app`, while the separate `keepers://auth-callback` account-authentication route remains intact. Native routing code does not prove public verification: the shipping certificate fingerprint, Apple Team ID, association documents, HTTPS responses, and physical-device behavior remain release gates. Manual code entry remains available when the verified-link infrastructure is unavailable.
- The family title is the first content beneath the wordmark and sits immediately above the presence sentence and attendance marks. It is rendered in Title Case at a lighter interface weight to match the Wheel's tracked labels, while its accessibility label keeps the natural-case family name (for example, visible `Rahman Family`, announced `Rahman family`). A small outlined question mark sits directly beside it inside a 48-logical-pixel, keyboard-focusable target labeled `How the Family Wheel works`; its dismissible app-owned sheet explains the static bubbles, capture action, five-photo unlock, foreground-only waiting-room presence, three-quarters threshold, and remaining primary destinations without changing any state. It has no segmented underline or header-side count. The Wheel does not duplicate the kept total. Archive begins with one noninteractive `Kept Forever` strip showing the complete unfiltered total; its five pastel format marks remain decorative until real per-entry format totals are available.
- Every app-owned painted text surface uses the bundled Modern Society face and Title Case presentation. Size, weight, line height, and tracking remain role-specific. The four approved launch phrases retain sentence case exactly. Presented memories use Title Case without mutating stored content; editable fields preserve typed casing, and spoken accessibility labels remain natural-case.
- The family cluster is a fixed, centered viewport beginning 16 logical pixels beneath the attendance row. It does not pan, drag, pinch-zoom, drift, or shift/scale a bubble before navigation. Current-member, relative, and Invite taps act directly without changing the composition.
- Every primary destination uses the same edge-to-edge white navigation bar in this order: Wheel, Memory Key, centered `+`, Archive, Settings. It extends through the bottom safe area and uses five equal hit regions, black iconography, and a small black current-destination mark; it has no capsule geometry, side margin, rounding, or shadow. The `+` opens the same capture flow from every main destination and never changes the selected route. Memory Key uses a key icon and is the sole owner of Capsule memories; there is no separate task-lock destination or approval preview. It separates task-locked assignments from ready/opened Capsules and exposes only safe metadata until readiness. Archive is the sole owner of the visible 48-pixel outlined `Random Memory` pill. Its draw pool is the current Person/Theme-filtered set of eligible kept entries; it avoids an immediate repeat when another eligible memory exists and disables honestly when that pool is empty. Settings exposes real local identity, encryption-at-rest, and device motion-policy information without fake controls. Onboarding, capture, memory viewing, member detail, and ceremony playback retain their contextual back or close behavior. The Weekly entry appears only on the Wheel. Fewer than five qualifying photos keeps its contrast-safe lock disabled and exposes no preview or bypass; five photos changes it to the softly glowing key and enables entry to the contextual waiting room regardless of family presence. The room counts the current member once plus only trusted nearby members, deduplicates the active roster, and requires `ceil(full family roster × 0.75)`. Below quorum it reports who is here and how many are needed without exposing a start action. At quorum it exposes exactly one `Start weekly experience` action. Merely entering or closing the room cannot create a payload lease, decrypt a memory, or enter the reel; only that final action may do so. A Capsule without a task is ready immediately for each current roster member. A task Capsule stays under `Locked by family` until that member confirms completion; a successful local encrypted update moves only their assignment to `Capsule` and enables the fail-closed Open memory path. The app does not imply remote completion or cross-device payload delivery where those capabilities are not implemented.
- Settings owns the current profile only. Its `Your identity` entry shows the saved avatar preview, member name, `Edit avatar`, and chevron, then pushes the full-screen profile editor without the global bottom navigation. Option selection changes only a local draft; save is explicit, and the successful config refreshes the same current-member avatar in both Settings and the Family Wheel.
- Each route owns one primary scroll surface. Safe areas and the virtual keyboard may not obscure actions.
- Important labels wrap; identity names ellipsize only where the full value remains in semantics.
- Every gesture-only action has an accessible semantics action or labeled non-gesture alternative without adding a competing camera-first button row.

## Overlays and feedback

- Flutter app-owned dialogs/sheets only; no platform-generic confirmation copy.
- Discarding an unsaved capture requires explicit Keep editing / Discard actions.
- Back from a dirty avatar draft requires the same explicit `Keep editing` / `Discard changes` choice. A successful avatar save returns to Settings; a failed save remains in the editor with its draft and Retry action intact.
- Routine status uses route-scoped `ScaffoldMessenger`; corrective errors stay inline.
- Reduced motion bypasses the Flutter cold-launch sequence on its first frame and replaces nonessential spatial travel with an opacity change of at most 100 ms. The shared background and family-bubble field are always static; a newly installed member may use only a short opacity arrival and never translates.
- Eligible reveal activation from its canonical owner uses the established 560 ms pastel flood where specified; reduced motion skips it and completes the action immediately. Weekly entry into the waiting room is not reveal activation and therefore does not flood; its quorum-approved `Start weekly experience` action owns the flood immediately before the live payload is requested. Moving Weekly to the Wheel, Random to Archive, and Capsule to Memory Key does not change their privacy, eligibility, task, or proximity rules.

## Family-code authorization and privacy

- The code is a permanent family locator, not an authentication factor or encryption key. It is eight Crockford Base32 symbols and is stored in lookup form only as a SHA-256 hash. Its display material is encrypted with the 32-byte family key and cached only in encrypted form.
- Every active member can view/share the current code and corresponding verified HTTPS link. Only the family creator can regenerate it. Regeneration invalidates the prior code/link and cancels old-version pending requests, while already-approved requests remain completable.
- A signed-in account with no current membership can preview only the family name and sanitized roster avatars, then submit one request. Possession of a code never exposes memories and never grants membership. The first release enforces one family per account and pending requests expire exactly seven days after creation.
- Any active member can approve or decline. The approving device encrypts the family key through X25519, HKDF-SHA-256, and AES-256-GCM to the requester's dedicated joining public key. Supabase receives public keys and ciphertext, never the joining private key, shared secret, or plaintext family key.
- The requester installs family/member keys, family row, roster, identity binding, and an exact completion marker locally before the completion RPC activates membership. Restart, resume, and reconnect reuse the same proposed member/key and recover an uncertain completion idempotently. A switched account cannot consume or delete another account's joining material.
- Plaintext family codes and full join URLs are permitted only at the explicit entry, clipboard, and operating-system share boundaries. They must not enter analytics, crash reports, API/proxy logs, diagnostics, screenshots, route restoration, or persistent display state. Errors and object diagnostics remain redacted.
- Account email sign-in and `keepers://auth-callback` remain separate authentication infrastructure. Email no longer addresses or transports a new family invitation. The legacy recipient-email invitation RPCs remain callable temporarily only for older app versions.
- PostgreSQL enforces per-account/family quotas and neutral cooldown behavior. A separately verified production ingress throttle by source IP and a privileged resolved-request purge schedule are mandatory release gates, not client features.

## Async and resilience

- Encrypted save is pessimistic: success appears only after blob finalization and SQL insertion.
- Duplicate saves are blocked by the existing capture controller.
- Avatar save is likewise pessimistic: controls are disabled while its one encrypted repository update is in flight, repeat activation cannot create a second update, and a recoverable failure preserves the complete draft for retry.
- Offline storage is canonical for memory content and is the durable roster cache. Capture and browsing continue without Supabase configuration or network access. Invite and Join instead expose honest configuration/network errors and never imply remote completion.
- Archive photo tiles request real encrypted payloads lazily only as their square enters the visible gallery range. A screen-local queue permits no more than three active preview decryptions, removes queued work when tile ownership ends, rejects source previews above 16 MiB, and decodes accepted images within a 512 × 512 fit bound. Decrypted photo bytes have one Archive visibility owner and are cleared when the tile scrolls away, a detail/capture route covers Archive, the app backgrounds, Archive is disposed, the destination or identity changes, or an in-flight load completes after release. Voice/text cards do not require photo decryption, and Archive never persists a plaintext thumbnail cache.
- A new installation with no local identity cannot create a local family while cloud authentication is unconfigured. It remains at the account gate with Retry. Every local identity also requires a current compatible account session; signed-out and mismatched sessions return to account recovery instead of exposing a stale family surface.
- New family setup writes its local identity with no account binding. Family-code owner bootstrap binds it only after the backend confirms that the authenticated account can own that exact family. If the initial roster read races ahead of that membership creation and receives `FORBIDDEN`, the first successful online code bootstrap triggers one bounded authoritative roster retry; it never loops on a persistent authorization failure.
- A roster refresh publishes only after its remote members have been upserted into SQLCipher. Refresh failure leaves cached members visible. This sprint's membership sync is append-only; removal and tombstones are explicitly out of scope.
- Family joining is pessimistic and stage-specific. Duplicate code load, authentication, preview, request, approve/decline, install, complete, refresh, share, and regenerate actions are blocked while in flight. A browser provider remains pending until its callback arrives or the user explicitly chooses another method; that action enables a replacement only after atomically clearing an unclaimed PKCE verifier. A callback that already claimed the verifier remains the sole in-flight sign-in. Every browser or email-link callback must match its opaque, single-use attempt marker; stale and replayed callbacks are ignored, and successful six-digit code entry retires the pending browser attempt. A cached authoritative code may remain visible through an ordinary network failure; an uncommitted candidate never becomes display state. Pending/approved state survives missed Realtime and process restart through authoritative refresh plus the exact completion marker.
- The Weekly presentation boundary accepts a local presence snapshot supplied in production by the foreground BLE waiting-room session. A phone advertises and scans only while its signed-in member has that room open; closing, starting, backgrounding, or changing identity stops the session and clears remote attendance. Family-keyed rotating service UUIDs expose no raw family/member identifiers, and each trusted nearby member expires independently after 15 seconds. Unavailable radios, future-dated or stale observations, duplicates, unknown members, and out-of-roster presence fail closed. The required count is `ceil(full family roster × 0.75)`, including the current member exactly once. The final Start revalidates both current-week photo eligibility and fresh quorum so a room left open across Monday, or a member who has just left, cannot reveal content. No Home action bypasses photo progress, nearby-presence quorum, or the final Start action.
- Failed capture preserves safe input and exposes retry/discard through the capture surface.

## Validation

- Existing Riverpod controllers own validation and error mapping.
- Validate on explicit action, then while correcting an errored field.
- Family-code input ignores ASCII spaces and hyphens, normalizes case, accepts paste, and rejects ambiguous or malformed symbols locally. Invalid, unknown, throttled, and superseded lookup outcomes expose the same neutral Family not found state. Account-email validation remains owned by the separate authentication flow.
